The film is untouched. Every number from 0 to 13.6 is the shipped one: the wash coming up over your own wallpaper, the studio credit, the shader dawn, the mark drawn by the travelling light, the bell on the third branch, the lockup holding its beat. Scrub it and compare against 04-first-launch-intro.html — they are the same frames.

What changed is that the film no longer closes at 13.8. That close was never part of the film's front half; it came forward when act two was cut, and it goes back where it was. In the space are two beats, and the surface in them is the product's own popover — not a picture of it, not a diagram of it. Four approaches to act two were tried and rejected, three of them by reaching for diagram vocabulary. This is the path the record named as untried: the real surface at real density, narrowed to the two beats that have something true to show.

The mark does not fly to the tray

It used to. The flight landed at a fixed point in a 1280 × 800 canvas that is stretched across whatever screen is actually there — so it arrived near the menu bar and never on the icon. A mark that lands beside its own slot is worse than one that never points at it, because the gap is the thing you notice. The walk to the corner went with it: both were act two's furniture, parking the mark so it could preside over four demo scenes. Act two is back and the flight is still not. The bug was never about having somewhere to fly to — it was that a canvas coordinate is not a screen coordinate, and letterboxing makes the error different on every display. What replaces it is a sentence that is true wherever the tray happens to be.

The tray does not exist yet, and the line still holds

startTray() runs after the intro in index.ts, so while these two beats play there is no icon in the menu bar at all. The pointer would be pressing an empty strip.

And the reorder is not a one-line move, which is the part worth knowing before someone promises it in a stand-up: TrayController is constructed with lifecycle, and lifecycle = getPlatformLifecycle(HEALTH_PORT) runs on the line after await playIntro(). Putting the tray up first means moving the lifecycle construction ahead of the film too — which means the launchd/CLI layer is live during it. That may well be fine; it is not free, and it is a decision rather than a tidy-up. The alternative is cheaper and worse: write the line in the future tense and let the pointer press nothing.

The sound was measured, not guessed

A spectrogram of Dia's audio shows four things: a low band alone for the first three seconds, one broadband noise swell from 3.3 to 7.5 peaking at 6.3, a single transient spike at 8.0, and then nothing but typing clicks. No music bed. This score has the same shape.

Nothing here is filmed

A generated sunrise came back photoreal and fought the brand. Dia's field is not a landscape either — it is a soft abstract light with no detail in it. Rendered in canvas it is a few kilobytes, resolution-independent, and it can be driven off the same clock as the score.

The scrim, not a blend

Compositing the light with screen would make every dark area transparent — the desktop would show through anything we drew. Dia dims rather than blends: the wallpaper stays behind, the light is painted over it.

The sequence is movable, and moving it changes what it says

Each beat is a segment with its own clock: everything inside it is written against AT.<key>, and the absolute starts are summed from one array after the film hands over. Reordering the sequence is reordering that array — the durations, the score's typing cues, the pointer's waypoints and the wash all follow, because none of them holds a number of its own. It replaced a chain of offsets (B2 = B1 + 5.2) where the score, the wash and the statements each kept their own copy of the same moment.

Reordering is not free of meaning, and not every order is available. These two beats only work in this order: the card has to be there before the register inside it can be the subject.

The character sits this one out

He is the dock icon and the marketing; he is not in the first twelve seconds. The intro is about the machine waking up, and the mark carries it. Video generation refused him anyway.
